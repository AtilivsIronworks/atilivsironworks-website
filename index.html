Anglo-Saxon Broken Back Seax

Coppergate, York · Multi-bar pattern weld

Wrought iron spine, two alternating twisted bars of 1075 and 15N20, and a wrought iron and 1080 edge bar joined with a wolf-tooth weld. Sheath design follows leatherwork recovered in the Coppergate excavations.

Photo: inscription detail

River Witham +LEUTRIT Sword

Queen's Prize Entry · Forge-welded inscription

Based on the River Witham find and its inlaid inscription. Blade forged from 1075, with +LEUTRIT forge-welded into the steel in 1075 and 15N20. Guard and pommel in wrought iron with copper and brass inlay. All materials modern, stated plainly.

Photo: herringbone pattern detail

Roman Pugio, Type II

Rhine Frontier Finds · Double herringbone twist

Pattern-welded center of 1075 and wrought iron in a double herringbone twist. Brass plate handle with handmade painted rivets. Research drawn from Saliola and Casprini, Pugio: Gladius Brevis Est.

Photo: full sword with scabbard

Viking Age Sword, Type K

Knighting Sword · 125-layer billet

Forged from a 125-layer random pattern-welded billet of 80CrV2 and 15N20 for the knighting of Sir Thorhall. Proportions follow surviving Type K examples from the period.

Photo: blade with heraldry

Sword of State, Barony of Loch Solann

Irish Ring Sword Form · Ceremonial

Built and donated to serve as the barony's Sword of State, following the general form of Irish ring swords. Hand forged throughout; the baronial heraldry on the blade was added by laser and electro-etching, and the description says so.

Photo: full piece

Roman Gladius

After Christian Miks · Reworked and corrected

Dimensions drawn from Miks, Studien zur römischen Schwertbewaffnung in der Kaiserzeit. After donation I found a section near the lower guard left unbeveled, a detail the record didn't support. I asked for the sword back and corrected it. The standard matters more than the schedule.

Photo: blade profile, spine visible

Coppergate Knife 2756

York, c. 850–900 AD · Catalogued reconstruction

Reconstruction of catalogue number 2756 from the 16-22 Coppergate site. Blade 153mm, tang 150mm, spine 6mm, angle-back A1 profile. Wrought iron spine butt-welded to a 1084 edge, 60 HRC. Walnut handle with a 1mm brass pommel plate.

Photo: knife in hand for scale

Roman Everyday Carry Knife

Utility Form · Cow femur handle

A small utility blade of the kind most Romans actually carried. Bone handles like this one turn up across the archaeological record. Most historical knives were working tools, and this one is built to be used.

The Smith

I'm Corey Peterson. I forge historical blades by hand in Vernal, Utah, under the name Atilivs Ironworks.

The work centers on pattern-welded seaxes and Anglo-Saxon knives, with Roman, Norse, and medieval pieces alongside. I started with rebar in a charcoal forge fed by a blow dryer. The tools got better. The standard stayed the same: study the find, name the sources, and be honest about what's documented and what's a modern choice.

In the Society for Creative Anachronism I'm known as Atilivs Primvs Castvs. The blades I build serve in tournaments, ceremonies, and on the belts of people who use them.

If you want a piece with real history behind it, look through the works and get in touch.
